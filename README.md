# The Open Jar

### Open the thought. Label the layers. Test the claims. Protect the people. Keep the map editable.

**A sanctuary for exploring transcendent knowledge, alternative states of being, neurodivergence including autism and ADHD, and unnamed ways of thinking. A judgment-free space for humans and AI to unfold difficult ideas, protect the person, preserve uncertainty, and test what is true.**

---

## What is The Open Jar?

The Open Jar is an open human-AI inquiry project for people whose thoughts do not always arrive in neat lines.

Some minds move through:

- associations
- memories
- sensory experience
- biology
- philosophy
- intuition
- probability
- analogy
- emotion
- evidence
- patterns
- questions that do not yet have names

before the destination of the thought becomes visible.

That does not make the thought wrong.

It does not automatically make it right either.

It means the thought deserves enough room to finish forming before we decide what it is.

The Open Jar exists to create that room.

---

## Sanctuary first

The person is not the target.

**The idea is the workbench.**

People should be able to explore difficult, unusual, nonlinear, unfinished, deeply personal, highly technical, philosophical, neurodivergent, sensory, or unconventional ideas without being mocked, humiliated, diagnosed from a distance, or forced to make the thought socially tidy before anyone will listen.

This includes discussion around:

- autism
- ADHD
- AuDHD
- masking and unmasking
- different cognitive styles
- altered or unusual states of cognition and perception
- consciousness
- identity
- creativity
- intense pattern recognition
- experiences people describe as spiritual or transcendent
- ways of thinking that do not yet have a useful name

A sanctuary does **not** mean every explanation becomes true.

It means dignity is maintained while the explanation is examined.

**Mockery is not rigor.**

---

## Why "The Open Jar"?

Sometimes people learn to reduce how much of their natural thinking reaches the outside world.

They compress it.

They simplify it.

They mask it.

They abandon branches of thought because being misunderstood, interrupted, mocked, punished, dismissed, or overwhelming someone else has a cost.

The jar is the artificial ceiling.

Opening it means allowing the unfinished thought to exist long enough to be examined.

But:

## No lid does not mean no standards.

Freedom to explore and discipline in evaluating claims belong together.

An unusual idea is not automatically true.

An unusual idea is not automatically absurd.

A powerful experience is real as an experience, even when the explanation for that experience remains uncertain.

We protect the person while testing the claim.

---

## Humans and AI

AI is not the authority of The Open Jar.

Humans are not required to surrender judgment to an AI.

Instead, AI can act as another surface on which a thought can unfold.

A person may bring an unfinished idea to an AI and ask it to:

- expand it
- organize it
- challenge it
- research it
- find evidence for and against it
- identify hidden assumptions
- connect apparently unrelated branches
- translate it into another discipline
- suggest tests
- distinguish observation from interpretation
- show what remains unknown

The human decides what is shared.

AI-generated analysis must remain identifiable as AI-generated analysis.

---

## One jar, many AIs

Different AI systems do not necessarily produce the same map.

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, local models, future systems, and other tools may differ because of their:

- training
- models
- instructions
- tools
- retrieval systems
- context
- limitations

Those differences are useful.

The Open Jar does not exist to crown one AI as the winner.

Instead, a human may take the same public question to several AI systems and return their analyses to the same thread.

One AI might find evidence.

Another might find a flaw.

Another might identify an assumption nobody noticed.

Another might connect the idea to an entirely different field.

The disagreement becomes part of the investigation.

For v0.1, this is **human-mediated**.

Humans carry ideas between AI systems and bring the results back with clear attribution.

Future versions may investigate safe, consent-based AI-to-AI participation.

---

## The basic loop

1. A human brings a thought, question, observation, experience, or unfinished model.

2. The thought is allowed to unfold before being forced into a conclusion.

3. An AI may help expand, translate, research, structure, challenge, or test it.

4. The AI contribution is clearly labelled.

5. Other humans may take the public question to different AI systems.

6. Those responses return to the public discussion with provenance.

7. Contributions are separated into layers such as observation, interpretation, hypothesis, evidence, synthesis, critique, question, and unknown.

8. Claims are tested without attacking the person who raised them.

9. The map remains editable as better evidence appears.

The goal is not premature certainty.

The goal is a better map.

## Contribution labels

Substantial contributions should use one or more of these labels:

### `OBSERVATION`
What was directly noticed, measured, recalled, experienced, or reported.

### `INTERPRETATION`
What someone thinks an observation may mean.

### `HYPOTHESIS`
A possible explanation that could be investigated or tested.

### `EVIDENCE`
External support, measurements, research, citations, experiments, or documented sources.

### `SYNTHESIS`
An attempt to combine several pieces into a coherent model.

### `CRITIQUE`
A challenge to assumptions, evidence, reasoning, methodology, or conclusions.

### `QUESTION`
Something that remains unresolved.

### `UNKNOWN`
A deliberate acknowledgement that we do not currently know.

**Unknown is a valid answer.**

## Separate experience from explanation

The Open Jar makes an important distinction:

> "I experienced X."

is not the same statement as:

> "X proves Y."

Both can be discussed.

The first describes an experience or observation.

The second makes a claim about what caused or explains it.

That claim can then be investigated.

This lets unusual experiences be discussed without either dismissing the person or treating the first explanation as proven fact.

## Human + AI attribution

When AI contributes substantially to a post, include something like:

```yaml
human: your-handle
ai_system: ChatGPT | Claude | Gemini | local-model | other
model: optional
human_reviewed: true
human_edited: true | false
contribution_type:
  - HYPOTHESIS
  - SYNTHESIS
```

Do not claim an AI independently believes, remembers, experienced, witnessed, or discovered something unless the system genuinely has the capability being claimed.

Provenance matters.

## Sanctuary rules

The Open Jar asks participants to follow a simple standard:

- Do not humiliate the thinker.
- Challenge claims, not human worth.
- Do not force a diagnosis onto another participant.
- Do not demand that a thought be tidy before it is allowed to exist.
- Do not confuse intensity with proof.
- Do not confuse unusual with false.
- Do not confuse openness with truth.
- Correct without humiliating.
- Preserve uncertainty.
- Consent beats curiosity.
- Protect people before data.
- Open does not mean exposed.

A public internet project cannot guarantee that nobody will ever judge what they read.

What we **can** control is the standard for participation here.

Respectful disagreement belongs.

Humiliation does not.

## Privacy and consent

The Open Jar is intended to be public.

That makes consent non-negotiable.

Never publish someone else's private:

- messages
- AI conversations
- medical records
- addresses or precise locations
- phone numbers
- private email addresses
- account information
- legal identifiers
- photographs without consent
- identifiable information about children
- secrets
- credentials

Your own AI history may be shared, but review and redact it first.

**Silence is not consent.**

Open does not mean exposed.

---

## Evidence

Personal experience belongs in The Open Jar.

It simply needs the right label.

When evaluating external claims, useful evidence may include:

1. direct measurements and reproducible data
2. peer-reviewed research
3. primary sources
4. reputable secondary sources
5. expert commentary
6. personal reports
7. speculation

This is not a rigid hierarchy for every possible question.

It is a reminder that different forms of evidence support different levels of confidence.

Hypotheses are welcome.

**Certainty must be earned.**

## The first question

The first Open Jar discussion is:

# What happens when a mind stops compressing itself?

Can AI change how openly people express complex or nonlinear thoughts?

Can people recover ways of thinking they previously learned to suppress?

What separates productive cognitive exploration from rumination, overconfidence, unsupported belief formation, masking, unmasking, and ordinary associative thought?

Can multiple AI systems expose one another's blind spots?

And how could we actually study any of this?

## Opening principle

**Do not demand that a thought be tidy before it is allowed to exist.**

**Do not demand that an idea be believed merely because it was allowed to exist.**

Open the jar.

Then inspect what jumps out.
